# PARTE A — LANÇAMENTOS

## A1. Macintosh — 24 jan. 1984 (assembleia de acionistas, Flint Center, Cupertino)

- **URL aberta:** https://www.folklore.org/Intro_Demo.html. É o relato de Andy Hertzfeld, que montou a demo, com o título "It Sure Is Great To Get Out Of That Bag!" **[P]** (memória de participante)
- **Cópia de apoio:** https://www.mactrast.com/2012/10/amazing-apple-anecdote-the-macintosh-demo/ (reproduz o mesmo texto) **[S]**

Trechos:
1. A ordem de Jobs à equipe, uma semana antes: "We need a demo for the intro! The Mac deserves to have a great demo for its first public showing." [P]
2. Jobs, ao ouvir o sintetizador de voz: "I want the Macintosh to be the first computer to introduce itself!" [P]
3. A fala do Mac no palco (roteiro de Steve Hayden, da Chiat-Day, segundo Hertzfeld): "Hello, I am Macintosh. It sure is great to get out of that bag!" [P]
4. "Unaccustomed as I am to public speaking, I'd like to share with you a maxim I thought of the first time I met an IBM mainframe: Never trust a computer that you can't lift!" [P]
5. "Obviously, I can talk, but right now I'd like to sit back and listen. So it is with considerable pride that I introduce a man who has been like a father to me... Steve Jobs!" [P]
6. Bastidor: a demo não cabia num Mac padrão, então usaram um protótipo de 512K: "so we decided to cheat a little (there were only two in existence at the time)". [P]

**Leitura:** o produto é o protagonista e se apresenta sozinho. O vilão aparece numa piada (o mainframe da IBM), e o atributo central (é leve e pessoal) é mostrado ao vivo: o Mac sai de uma bolsa. Jobs exigiu a demo como parte do produto, mesmo com a equipe exausta.

## A2. iMac — 6 maio 1998 (Flint Center)

- **URL aberta:** https://www.allaboutstevejobs.com/videos/keynotes/imac_introduction_1998 (transcrição do vídeo) **[S]**

Trechos:
1. Ele abre com o estado da empresa, não com o produto: "I'm really pleased to report to you today that Apple is back on track." [00:00:13] [S]
2. A estratégia em quatro quadrantes: "We've got to make four great products and we can meet the needs of what our customers are telling us they want." [S]
3. O nome e a promessa: "iMac comes from the marriage of the excitement of the internet with the simplicity of Macintosh." [00:15:55] [S]
4. O que o "i" quer dizer: "we are targeting this for the number one use that consumers tell us they want a computer for, which is to get on the internet simply and fast." [S]
5. O vilão, com os PCs de consumo da época: "The first is they're really slow... Second is they've all got pretty crummy displays on them." [00:17:15] [S]
6. "Likely no networking on them... And these things are uhhh-gly." [00:17:35] [S]
7. "This is iMac. The whole thing is translucent. You can see into it. It's so cool." [S]
8. "The back of this thing looks better than the front of the other guys, by the way." [S]
9. "It looks like it's from another planet. And a good planet. A planet with better designers." [S]
10. A demo ao vivo é uma corrida contra um Compaq: "All right. Three. Two. One. Go." [00:25:57] [S]
11. O preço, dito como pacote fechado: "we decided to price all of this all together in this package, complete, nothing else to buy. $1,299." [S]
12. No mesmo evento, ao apresentar o novo PowerBook G3 [00:08:45–00:09:03], aparece a semente do "one more thing": "And, oh, one other thing. It eats Pentium notebooks for lunch too." [S]

**Leitura:** a sequência é contexto (a empresa voltou), depois estratégia (quatro produtos), depois o problema do concorrente (lento, feio, sem rede), depois o produto, a demo comparativa e o preço único, com "nothing else to buy". O nome carrega o caso de uso: i = internet.

## A3. iPod — 23 out. 2001

- **URL aberta:** https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2001/10/23Apple-Presents-iPod/ (press release da Apple) **[P]**
- **Não consegui a transcrição do keynote:** a página https://www.allaboutstevejobs.com/videos/keynotes/ipod_introduction_2001 só tem um resumo, sem o texto da fala.

Trechos:
1. Subtítulo do release: "Ultra-Portable MP3 Music Player Puts 1,000 Songs in Your Pocket" [P]
2. "With iPod, Apple has invented a whole new category of digital music player that lets you put your entire music collection in your pocket and listen to it wherever you go," said Steve Jobs... "With iPod, listening to music will never be the same again." [P]
3. O número a decorar e a comparação: "iPod stores up to 1,000 CD-quality songs on its super-thin 5 GB hard drive" [P]
4. "iPod's built-in FireWire® port lets you download an entire CD into iPod in under 10 seconds and 1,000 songs in less than 10 minutes—30 times faster than USB-based players." [P]
5. Preço: "for a suggested retail price of $399 (US)" [P]

**Ressalva:** a frase "1,000 songs in your pocket" está verificada como manchete do release. Não verifiquei que Jobs a disse no palco com essas palavras. Fonte secundária, que não abri (só vi o resumo da busca): https://www.latercera.com/piensa-digital/noticia/20-anos-del-ipod-mil-canciones-en-tu-bolsillo/TKB47IMH6RA3ZOG4VHBWOCI6DI/. Segundo esse resumo, a versão "I'll put a thousand songs in your pocket" é cena do filme de 2015.

**Leitura:** um benefício só, medido em unidade do usuário (músicas, não gigabytes), e no bolso, uma imagem física. A especificação técnica (5 GB, FireWire) aparece só como prova do benefício.

## A5. iPhone — Macworld, 9 jan. 2007

- **URL aberta:** https://www.allaboutstevejobs.com/videos/keynotes/macworld_2007 (transcrição do vídeo) **[S]**
- O WebFetch da transcrição da Singju Post (https://singjupost.com/steve-jobs-iphone-2007-presentation-full-transcript/) falhou (conexão recusada).

Trechos:
1. "This is a day I've been looking forward to for two and a half years." [S]
2. "Every once in a while, a revolutionary product comes along that changes everything." [00:26:33] [S]
3. A linhagem: "In 1984, we introduced the Macintosh. It didn't just change Apple. It changed the whole computer industry." [00:27:00] / "In 2001, we introduced the first iPod... It changed the entire music industry." [00:27:21] [S]
4. A regra de três: "Well, today we're introducing three revolutionary products of this class." [00:27:38] [S]
5. "So - three things. A widescreen iPod with touch controls, a revolutionary mobile phone and a breakthrough internet communications device. An iPod, a phone and an internet communicator. An iPod, a phone. Are you getting it? These are not three separate devices. This is one device. And we are calling it iPhone." [00:28:32] [S]
6. "Today - today, Apple is going to reinvent the phone." [00:28:32] [S]
7. O vilão: "The most advanced phones are called smartphones, so they say... And they all have these plastic little keyboards on them. And the problem is that they're not so smart and they're not so easy to use." [00:30:15] [S]
8. "They all have these keyboards that are there, whether you need them or not to be there. And they all have these control buttons that are fixed in plastic and are the same for every application." [S]
9. "And what happens if you think of a great idea six months from now? You can't run around and add a button to these things, they're already shipped." [S]
10. A solução vem de uma coisa que a Apple já tinha resolvido: "It turns out, we have solved it! We solved it in computers 20 years ago. We solved it with a bitmap screen that could display anything we want." [S]
11. "Well, what we're going to do is get rid of all these buttons and just make a giant screen." [S]
12. "No. Who wants a stylus? You have to get them, and put them away, and you lose them. Yuck. Nobody wants a stylus." [00:33:05] [S]
13. "We're going to use the best pointing device in the world. We're going to use a pointing device that we're all born with. We're born with ten of them. We're gonna use our fingers." [S]
14. "First was the mouse. The second was the click wheel. And now we're going to bring multi-touch to the market. And each of these revolutionary user interfaces has made possible a revolutionary product: the Mac, the iPod, and now the iPhone." [S]
15. "Software that's at least five years ahead of what's on any other phone." [S]
16. Ele cita Alan Kay: "People who are really serious about software should make their own hardware." [S]
17. A demo ao vivo com o trote: "Yes, I'd like to order 4,000 lattes to go, please. No, just kidding. Wrong number." [S]
18. O número: "What this tells you is that 1% market share equals 10 million units." [00:43:21] [S]
19. O nome da empresa muda junto: "we're dropping the "Computer" from our name. And from this day forward, we're going to be known as Apple Incorporated". [S]
20. O fecho: "There's an old Wayne Gretzky quote that I love. I skate to where the puck is going to be, not where it has been." [S]

**Ressalva:** a transcrição usada não tem nenhuma frase com "one more thing" neste keynote.

**Leitura:** é o modelo mais completo da técnica. Primeiro uma linhagem de produtos que mudaram tudo, para criar expectativa. Depois três itens que viram um só, com o "are you getting it?" para envolver a plateia. O vilão tem nome (Motorola Q, BlackBerry, Palm Treo, Nokia E62, que ele chama de "the usual suspects"). A solução é derivada de um princípio que a Apple já tinha validado (tela bitmap e um dispositivo de apontar). Cada objeção é eliminada em voz alta ("Who wants a stylus?"), a demo é longa e ao vivo, e o fecho é um número modesto e memorável (1% = 10 milhões).

## A6. MacBook Air — Macworld, 15 jan. 2008

- **URL aberta:** https://www.engadget.com/2008/01/15/live-from-macworld-2008-steve-jobs-keynote/ (liveblog) **[P*]**
- **URL aberta:** https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2008/01/15Apple-Introduces-MacBook-Air-The-Worlds-Thinnest-Notebook/ **[P]**
- **URL aberta:** https://laughingsquid.com/steve-jobs-macworld-2008-keynote-live-coverage-announcements/ (cobertura da época) **[P*]**

Trechos:
1. A abertura: "There is clearly something in the air today." [P*]
2. "What is the MacBook Air? In a sentence, it's the world's thinnest notebook." [P*]
3. Narrado pelo liveblog: "It's so thin it fits in a manilla envelope. Let me show it to you now. / He pulls out a manilla envelope. Huge applause." [P*]
4. "It fits inside the evenlope, it's that small... World's thinnest notebook." [P*] (com o erro de digitação do liveblog)
5. Do release: "We've built the world's thinnest notebook—without sacrificing a full-size keyboard or a full-size 13-inch display," said Steve Jobs [P]

## A7. iPad — 27 jan. 2010

- **URL aberta:** https://spokesman.com/stories/2010/jan/28/ipad-makes-its-debut (matéria da Associated Press de 28 jan. 2010, assinada por Jessica Mintz e Rachel Metz) **[P]**
- **URL aberta:** https://www.macstories.net/stories/on-ipads-and-personal-computers-a-post-pc-retrospective/ (reproduz a fala de abertura) **[S]**
- **URL aberta:** https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2010/01/27Apple-Launches-iPad/ **[P]**

Trechos:
1. Na AP: "In order to really create a new category of devices, those devices are going to have to be far better at doing some key tasks," Jobs said. "We think we've got the goods. We think we've done it." [P]
2. Na AP: "so much more intimate than a laptop and so much more capable than a smart phone." [P]
3. Abertura completa, na MacStories: "Everybody uses a laptop and a smartphone. And a question has arisen lately: is there room for a third category of device in the middle? Something that's between a laptop and a smartphone... The bar's pretty high. In order to really create a new category of devices, those devices are going to have to be far better at doing some key tasks. Better than a laptop. Better than a smartphone." [S]
4. O vilão na mesma fala: "Now, some people have thought…that's a netbook. The problem is, netbooks aren't better at anything. They're slow, they have low quality displays and they run clunky old PC software... They're just cheap laptops. We don't think they're a new category of device." [S]
5. Do release: "iPad is our most advanced technology in a magical and revolutionary device at an unbelievable price," said Steve Jobs [P]
6. Do release: "...starting at the breakthrough price of just $499." [P]

**Leitura:** antes de mostrar o produto, ele define o critério da categoria ("far better at doing some key tasks") e derruba o concorrente com esse mesmo critério: o netbook não é melhor em nada, é só mais barato. Na mesma fala aparecem o teste de produto (ser muito melhor em poucas tarefas-chave) e a narrativa (o terceiro dispositivo).

# PARTE B — FRACASSOS

## B1. Apple III (1980) e Lisa (1983)

- **URL aberta:** https://allaboutstevejobs.com/verbatim/interviews/playboy_1985 (Playboy, entrevista de David Sheff, publicada em 1 fev. 1985, reproduzida pelo site "for documentary purposes") **[S]**
- **URL aberta:** https://allaboutstevejobs.com/verbatim/interviews/smithsonian_interview_1995 (entrevista de história oral de 20 abr. 1995, feita por Daniel Morrow; transcrição do site) **[S]**
- O original do Smithsonian (https://americanhistory.si.edu/comphist/sj1.html) estava fora do ar ("temporarily unavailable").

Trechos:
1. A pergunta da Playboy registra o fato: "You recalled the first 14,000, and even the revised Apple III never took off. How much was lost on Apple III?" A resposta de Jobs: "Infinite, incalculable amounts. I think if the III had been more successful, IBM would have had a much harder time entering the market place. But that's life. I think we emerged from that experience much stronger." [S]
2. Sobre o Lisa: "First of all, it was too expensive—about ten grand. We had gotten Fortune 500-itis, trying to sell to those huge corporations, when our roots were selling to people." [S]
3. "There were other problems: late shipping; the software didn't come together in the end as well as we hoped and we lost a lot of momentum." [S]
4. "...plus another strategic mistake we made—deciding to sell Lisa only through about 150 dealers, which was absolutely foolish on our part—meant it was a very costly mistake." [S]
5. "We decided to hire people we thought were marketing and management experts... the things the so-called professionals knew were almost detriments to their success in this new way of looking at business." [S]
6. "There was a big conflict in the Lisa group between the people who wanted, in essence, to build something like Macintosh and the people hired from Hewlett-Packard and other companies who brought with them a perspective of larger machines, corporate sales." [S]
7. "It's true: We expressed very high hopes for Lisa and we were wrong." [S]
8. Em 1995: "It happened on the Apple II and it happened on the Lisa, although there were other problems with the Lisa that caused it to be a market failure; and then it happened again big time on the Macintosh." [S]

**O que deu errado (segundo o próprio Jobs, em 1985):** preço (cerca de US$ 10 mil), público errado (Fortune 500 em vez de pessoas), atraso, software que não ficou pronto, distribuição restrita a 150 revendas e uma equipe sem a mesma visão.
**Leitura (lição de produto):** preço e canal fazem parte do produto. Um time que não compartilha a visão produz outro produto. A resposta dele foi sair com um grupo pequeno ("go back to the garage") para fazer o Mac.

## B2. Saída da Apple (1985) e o que ele disse em Stanford (2005)

- **URL aberta:** https://www.npr.org/transcripts/141122856 (transcrição da NPR, de out. 2011, que reproduz o áudio do discurso) **[S]**

## B3. NeXT — hardware abandonado, foco em software (NeXTSTEP / WebObjects)

- **URL aberta:** https://www.wired.com/1996/02/jobs-2/ ("Steve Jobs: The Next Insanely Great Thing", de Gary Wolf, Wired, 1 fev. 1996) **[P]**
- **URL aberta:** https://allaboutstevejobs.com/verbatim/interviews/smithsonian_interview_1995 **[S]**

Trechos:
1. Jobs, em 1995: "But we made a mistake which was to try to follow the same formula we did at Apple, to make the whole widget. But the market was changing. The industry was changing. The scale was changing." [S]
2. "We thought we would be the last one that made it, but we were wrong. We were the first one that didn't." [S]
3. "We certainly made our fair share of mistakes, but in the end I think we should have taken a bit longer to realize the world was changing and just gone on to be a software company right off the bat." [S]
4. "The company last year recorded its first profit in its nine year history, and sold fifty million dollars worth of software." [S]
5. Fato, nas palavras da Wired (narrador, não Jobs): "After burning through hundreds of millions of dollars from investors, the company abandoned the production of computers, focusing instead on the sale and development of its Nextstep operating system and on extensions into object-oriented technology." [P]
6. Jobs, na Wired: "Our new product, WebObjects, allows you to write Web applications 10 times faster." [P]
7. Jobs, na Wired: "The end result of objects---this repackaging of software---is that we can develop applications with only about 10 to 20 percent of the software development required any other way." [P]
8. Jobs, na Wired, sobre a indústria: "The desktop computer industry is dead... Apple lost." [P]

**O que deu errado:** repetir a fórmula do "whole widget" (hardware próprio) num mercado que tinha mudado de escala.
**Leitura (lição):** quando a premissa muda, mude de produto e mantenha o ativo de verdade, que aqui era o software. O próprio Jobs diz que deveria ter mudado antes. Em 2007 a tese volta invertida (A5.16, Alan Kay): quem leva software a sério deve fazer o próprio hardware, quando a escala permite.

**Leitura (lição):** o desenho venceu e o caso de uso perdeu. Os clientes escolheram o produto da própria Apple que tinha mais capacidade pelo mesmo dinheiro. A Apple cortou rápido (cerca de 1 ano) e disse publicamente por quê.

## B5. ROKR E1 — o "iTunes phone" da Motorola (set. 2005)

- **URL aberta:** https://www.frankrose.com/reporting/battle-for-the-soul-of-the-mp3-phone/ (Frank Rose, datado de 1 nov. 2005 no site do autor; o veículo original não está confirmado na página) **[P*]**
- **URL aberta:** https://m.gsmarena.com/flashback_the_motorola_rokr_e1_was_a_dud_but_it_paved_the_way_for_the_iphone-news-38934.php (retrospectiva) **[S]**
- **URL aberta:** https://www.mobhappy.com/2005/09/21/rokr-an-apple-learning-experience/ (blog de 21 set. 2005 que cita o MyPhoneRocks) **[S]**
- O press release da Apple de 7 set. 2005 (https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2005/09/07Apple-Motorola-Cingular-Launch-World-s-First-Mobile-Phone-with-iTunes/) dá "Page Not Found".

Trechos:
1. A demo que falhou no palco, segundo Rose: "Well," he said, looking perplexed, "I'm supposed to be able to resume the music right back to where it was. . . ." Then: "Oops! I hit the wrong button." [P*]
2. O limite imposto pelo DRM, segundo Rose: "It couldn't play music from any major online store but iTunes. It couldn't hold more than 100 songs." [P*]

**Leitura (lição):** a Apple não controlava o hardware, o software nem o canal, então não controlava a experiência. Isso conversa com A5.16 e com a "Cook Doctrine" citada por Gruber (abaixo). Dezesseis meses depois veio o iPhone, com a Apple controlando o aparelho inteiro.

## B6. MobileMe (lançado em 9 jul. 2008)

- **URL aberta:** https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/archives/2008/08/05/steve-jobs-mobileme-launched-too-early (trecho do e-mail interno de Jobs de 4 ago. 2008, que vazou para a Ars Technica) **[P*]**
- **URL aberta:** https://www.theregister.com/2008/08/05/apple_not_perfect/ **[P*]**
- **URL aberta:** https://techcrunch.com/2008/08/05/steve-jobs-admits-mobileme-not-up-to-apples-standards **[P*]**
- **URL aberta:** https://fortune.com/2011/05/09/inside-apple/ (Adam Lashinsky, Fortune, 9 maio 2011; a reunião é contada por um participante) **[P*]**
- **URL aberta:** https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2008/06/09Apple-Introduces-MobileMe-Internet-Service/ **[P]**
- O original da Ars Technica (https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2008/08/steve-jobs-on-mobileme-the-full-e-mail/) pede CAPTCHA.

Trechos:
1. A promessa do lançamento: "Think of MobileMe as 'Exchange for the rest of us,'" said Steve Jobs [P]
2. O e-mail: "It was a mistake to launch MobileMe at the same time as iPhone 3G, iPhone 2.0 software and the App Store. We all had more than enough to do, and MobileMe could have been delayed without consequence." [P*]
3. "The MobileMe launch clearly demonstrates that we have more to learn about Internet service. And learn we will. The vision of MobileMe is both exciting and ambitious, and we will press on to make it a service we are all proud of by the end of this year." [P*]
4. Como a imprensa resumiu o e-mail: the MobileMe launch was "not up to Apple's standards." [P*]
5. A reunião com a equipe, segundo um participante ouvido pela Fortune: "Can anyone tell me what MobileMe is supposed to do?" Having received a satisfactory answer, he continued, "So why the fuck doesn't it do that?" [P*]
6. Na mesma reunião: "You've tarnished Apple's reputation," he told them. "You should hate each other for having let each other down." [P*]
7. Ainda na Fortune: "On the spot, Jobs named a new executive to run the group." [P*] O TechCrunch registra a consequência: "the MobileMe development team will now report to the same guy in charge of iTunes and the App Store." [P*]

**O que deu errado:** lançar tudo junto, um serviço de nuvem que não fazia o que prometia e uma operação de internet imatura.
**Leitura (lição):** a pergunta de produto é "o que isto deveria fazer?" e "por que não faz?". Adiar teria custado pouco e lançar quebrado custou a reputação. Ele também nomeou um responsável único na hora.

## B7. Antennagate — iPhone 4 (coletiva de 16 jul. 2010)

- **URL aberta:** https://www.engadget.com/2010/07/16/live-from-apples-iphone-4-press-conference/ (liveblog, em ordem cronológica reversa) **[P*]**
- **URL aberta:** https://techcrunch.com/2010/07/16/steve-jobs-were-not-perfect/ **[P*]**
- **URL aberta:** https://www.techradar.com/news/computing/apple/steve-jobs-we-re-not-perfect-phones-aren-t-perfect-703819 **[P*]**
- **URL aberta:** https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2010/07/02Letter-from-Apple-Regarding-iPhone-4/ (carta da Apple de 2 jul. 2010, assinada pela empresa) **[P]**
- **URL aberta:** https://daringfireball.net/2026/09/iphone_4_antennagate_q_and_a (Gruber sobre o vídeo do Q&A que apareceu em set. 2026) **[P*]**
- A transcrição da allaboutstevejobs (https://www.allaboutstevejobs.com/videos/keynotes/antennagate_2010) só tem um resumo. A versão exata "We're not perfect. Phones are not perfect. We all know that. But we want to make our users happy" aparece só em resumos de busca (Scott Adams via MacDailyNews), e a página da MacDailyNews não abriu com conteúdo. Por isso uso as versões abaixo.

Trechos:
1. Abertura, no Engadget: "You know, we're not perfect. We know that, you know that. And phones aren't perfect either." [P*]
2. "But we want to make all of our users happy. If you don't know that about Apple, you don't know Apple. We love making our users happy." [P*]
3. Na TechRadar: "We're not perfect, phones aren't perfect. But we want to make all our users happy," [P*]
4. "We've been working on this for just 22 days. We are an engineering company, and we want to find out what the real problem is." [P*]
5. "...we haven't figured out a way around the laws of physics yet." [P*]
6. "This is life in the smartphone world. Phones aren't perfect. It's a challenge for the whole industry. Every phone has weak spots." [P*]
7. Os dados: "So what percentage have called AppleCare? 0.55% Just one half of one percent." / "...it's 1.7% — less than a third of the 3GS returns." / "the iPhone 4 drops less than one additional call per 100 than the 3GS." [P*]
8. "Now when we look at this data, it's hard to escape the conclusion that there is a problem, but that problem is affecting a very small number of users." [P*]
9. A solução: "Okay — we'll give you a free case." / "And if you're not happy, you can bring the phone back. We'll give you a full refund within 30 days. No restocking fee." [P*]
10. Antes da coletiva, a carta de 2 jul.: "Upon investigation, we were stunned to find that the formula we use to calculate how many bars of signal strength to display is totally wrong." [P]
11. Na mesma carta: "Since this mistake has been present since the original iPhone, this software update will also be available for the iPhone 3GS and iPhone 3G." [P]
12. Gruber cita Jobs no Q&A: "We didn't want to get into any business where we didn't own or control the primary technology." / "Because if somebody else owns the primary technology, they're going to beat you in the end." [P*] (Gruber liga essas falas à chamada "Cook Doctrine")

**O que deu errado:** a antena externa perdia sinal com certa pegada, o indicador de barras era calculado errado e o efeito ficou viral com o vídeo do Gizmodo.
**Leitura (lição):** a crise foi tratada como lançamento, com roteiro: admitir ("we're not perfect"), relativizar com dados próprios (0.55%, 1.7%, menos de uma ligação a mais por 100), mostrar o problema nos concorrentes ao vivo, oferecer remédio concreto (capa grátis e reembolso) e reafirmar o valor ("we love making our users happy"). Só que parte da imprensa leu isso como pouco remorso, e por isso não é um modelo sem custo.

## B8. Síntese dos fracassos (leitura)

| Fracasso | Causa (nas palavras ou nos dados das fontes) | Lição de produto (leitura) |
|---|---|---|
| Apple III | recall de 14.000 unidades; "never took off" | qualidade de engenharia é pré-requisito do lançamento |
| Lisa | "too expensive", "Fortune 500-itis", atraso, 150 revendas | preço, público e canal fazem parte do produto |
| Saída em 1985 | "our visions of the future began to diverge" | o trabalho de fundo (NeXT) vira o próximo ativo |
| NeXT | "make the whole widget" num mercado que mudou | mudar cedo para onde está o valor (software) |
| G4 Cube | clientes preferiram a torre (Schiller) | desenho não compensa um caso de uso fraco; cortar rápido |
| ROKR | 100 músicas, DRM, canal da operadora | sem controle de ponta a ponta não há experiência |
| MobileMe | "launch... at the same time", "more to learn" | não lançar o que não faz o que promete; um dono único |
| Antennagate | antena + algoritmo de barras "totally wrong" | admitir, medir, remediar e reafirmar o valor |
